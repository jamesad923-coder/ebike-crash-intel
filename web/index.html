Crash Atlas

U.S. Pedalcyclist & E-Bike Crash Intelligence — an open-source dashboard built to help riders, parents, planners, and journalists see real patterns in bicycle and micromobility crashes, without inventing certainty the data doesn't support.

What's in here

  • Fatalities (FARS) — a map of pedalcyclist fatalities in fatal motor-vehicle crashes, with NHTSA's pre-crash-action coding. Federal, annual, lags ~1-2 years.
  • Injuries (NEISS) — national ED-visit estimates for micromobility injuries, broken out by device, age, and diagnosis, with a confirmed e-bike slice.
  • Recent reports (news) — automatically detected crash mentions from the last 7 days, age-band/device/outcome only, tagged corroborated vs. single-source.
  • Risk & Prevention — an interpretable (no opaque ML) state-level risk ranking and contributing-factor breakdown, each paired with a sourced prevention lever.
  • City Pilots — ward-level crash data and bikeshare exposure context for Washington, DC and Chicago, verified independently per city.

On the data

Every view discloses its own limitations directly in the UI — sample sizes, what a dataset can and can't claim, and where estimates stand in for real counts. Counts are not adjusted for ridership unless explicitly labeled as a rate.

Read this first. These are weighted national estimates from a sample of ~100 hospital EDs, not raw counts and not from this map's data. Recent Reports

Auto-detected from news coverage · unverified beyond automated checks

Read this first. These come from an automated news scan (GDELT), not a federal dataset. Each report is tagged corroborated (2+ independent outlets agree) or single-source (one outlet only) — neither means confirmed by this project. Early news can be wrong about cause or severity. Location, age, device, and outcome are extracted automatically by keyword/pattern matching, not read by a person. We show city/county + age band only — never a name or exact age, even when the source article includes one. Click through to the source(s) to verify before treating any single report as fact. A ⚠ throttle vehicle? badge means the article mentions a throttle-capable vehicle (e.g. Sur-Ron) that may not legally be a Class 1-3 e-bike at all — federal data has no e-bike class field, so this is a text-keyword flag, not a verified classification.

Risk & Prevention

Interpretable patterns, not a black-box prediction · every number shows its sample size

Read this first. Nothing here is machine-learning prediction — it's interpretable cross-tabs and a rate adjustment, fully auditable. The state ranking divides FARS fatalities by ACS commute-cycling data — a real but limited proxy that misses recreational, delivery, and minors' riding (the population most central to e-bikes). Cluster flags are raw fatality counts in an area, not exposure-adjusted risk — busier places show up partly because more people ride there. Prevention levers are citations from FHWA/NACTO/NHTSA guidance, not claims our data proves they work.

City Pilots

City-level deep-dives — verified independently per city, not assumed to generalize

Washington, DC

First city-level deep-dive · proof of concept for expanding beyond national data

Read this first. Ward crash counts below cover a fixed, recent window — not all-time. DC's source dataset actually goes back to 1996, but reporting was sparse and inconsistent before 2016; mixing 30 uneven years into one ward total would make a ward look worse partly because it's been recorded longer, not because it's more dangerous now. Counts are also not exposure-adjusted — we don't have per-ward ridership data, so this doesn't tell you a ward is more dangerous PER RIDER, only that more crashes are documented there. DC's data has no e-bike-specific flag either — same gap as the federal sources. The Capital Bikeshare e-bike trip context below is genuinely e-bike-specific (rare for this project!) but covers only Capital Bikeshare's own fleet, not personally-owned e-bikes — shown as citywide context, not blended into the ward numbers. "Near bike lane" means within ~30m of an OpenStreetMap-mapped cycleway — infrastructure context, not a cause: a crash near a bike lane isn't necessarily caused by its presence or absence.

Boston

Third city deep-dive · Verified independently per city, not assumed from DC or Chicago

Read this first. District crash counts cover a fixed recent window. Source is the City of Boston Vision Zero Crash Records (data.boston.gov). As of 2026 Boston's public crash file dropped its injury-severity field entirely — no more "Fatal injury" vs "Non-fatal injury" at all. The Fatal count below is instead an approximate match against Boston's separate Vision Zero Fatality Records file, joined by nearest timestamp (within 6h) and location (within ~1km) since the two files share no common id — treat it as a lower bound, and treat "Injury" as "not matched as fatal," not a confirmed injury. No speeding flag and no e-bike-specific flag (same gap as every other city in this project). Districts are assigned via point-in-polygon against Boston City Council District boundaries — no district field in the crash records directly. Bikeshare exposure is Bluebikes only, same caveat as DC: covers their fleet, not personally-owned bikes.

Chicago

Second city deep-dive · verified independently, not assumed from DC's pattern

Read this first. Ward crash counts cover a fixed recent window — Chicago's full data goes back to 2015, but we used a window matching DC's in length as a consistency choice, not because we separately verified Chicago's older reporting the way DC's pre-2016 gap was checked. Counts are not exposure-adjusted on their own — see the ward-exposure section below for that, which (like DC) compares ALL cyclists' crashes to Divvy's fleet only, NOT a true risk-per-rider rate. The ward table has TWO different bike-lane fields, shown separately on purpose: "Reported in lane" is what the police officer recorded at the scene, and "Near mapped lane" is the same ~30m OpenStreetMap-proximity estimate built for DC. They can disagree — a real lane might not be mapped on OSM yet, or vice versa. Helmet use is also directly police-reported.

New York City

Fourth city deep-dive · Verified independently per city, not assumed from DC or Chicago

Read this first. Borough crash counts cover a fixed recent window. Source is the NYC Motor Vehicle Collisions dataset (NYPD, data.cityofnewyork.us), filtered to crashes involving at least one cyclist injury or fatality. Borough is taken directly from the NYPD record — no spatial join needed. No e-bike-specific flag in the crash data. Bikeshare exposure uses Citi Bike only (excludes Jersey City files); same fleet-only caveat as every other city. Crash points without a reported borough are excluded from borough-level tables but are still shown on the map if they have coordinates.
