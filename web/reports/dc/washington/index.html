Executive summary

  • 12 cyclist fatalities recorded in federal crash data (FARS) for Washington, 2019–2024, including 1 minors (under 18).
  • Recorded fatalities have risen: the last two years average 2.0/yr vs 1.0/yr in the first two.
  • The most common crash type coded by reporting officers (PBCAT): “Other / Unusual Circumstances” (2 of 12) — a pattern, not a fault judgment.
  • District of Columbia recorded 12 pedalcyclist fatalities statewide (#43 by raw count — counts, not rates).
  • City-level crash concentration screening of 2,712 recorded bicyclist crashes flags 20 grid locations, 20 persistent across both halves of the data window — the locations most likely to reflect roadway conditions rather than chance.

Crash concentration screening (city open data)

Screening of 2,712 recorded bicyclist crashes (2022–2026, city open data) across ~250m grid cells — the standard first step of a High-Injury Network analysis. Persistent means concentrated in both halves of the window (≥2 crashes in each): those locations most likely reflect roadway conditions rather than chance, and are where systemic-safety practice directs engineering attention first. 20 of the top 20 locations are persistent.

How to read this honestly: this screening is count-based and not exposure-adjusted — busy corridors rank high partly because more people ride there, and a high-count location can be the safest place per rider in the city. It identifies where to look, not what to conclude. It supports engineering judgment; it never replaces it.

State context

District of Columbia recorded 12 pedalcyclist fatalities in FARS 2019–2024, ranking #43 among states by raw count. Raw counts favor large states: more residents, more riding, more exposure.

Adjusted for bike-commute share (a real but imperfect exposure proxy from ACS commute data — it misses recreational, delivery, and minors' riding), District of Columbia ranks #51 at 4.93 fatalities per 10,000 bike commuters (raw-count rank #42). The gap between the two ranks is itself informative.

What this data can and cannot say

  • Counts, not rates. No city-level ridership denominator exists; a higher count can simply mean more people ride.
  • Fatal motor-vehicle crashes only in the federal record — single-bike falls and non-fatal injuries (the majority of e-bike ED visits) are absent from FARS entirely.
  • E-bikes cannot be separated from conventional bicycles in federal crash data. Only CPSC's NEISS isolates them (2024 onward), as national emergency-department estimates that cannot be mapped to a city.
  • Only ~two-thirds of federal records are coded to a specific city — city counts are a floor, not a ceiling.
  • Crash-type codes describe what the report found, not fault.

Methodology & sources

Fatality data: NHTSA Fatality Analysis Reporting System (FARS), national files 2019–2024, pedalcyclist person types, age-banded (never exact ages). City crash screening: municipal open crash data via the Crash Atlas pilot pipeline, ~250m grid cells, persistence checked across window halves. State exposure adjustment: ACS bike-commute share. Full methodology, source documentation, and all code are public: https://github.com/jamesad923-coder/ebike-crash-intel — every number in this report can be independently reproduced.
