Executive summary

  • 45 cyclist fatalities recorded in federal crash data (FARS) for Tucson, 2019–2024, including 3 minors (under 18).
  • Recorded fatalities have risen: the last two years average 8.5/yr vs 6.0/yr in the first two.
  • The most common crash type coded by reporting officers (PBCAT): “Motorist Overtaking Bicyclist” (8 of 45) — a pattern, not a fault judgment.
  • Arizona recorded 281 pedalcyclist fatalities statewide (#5 by raw count — counts, not rates).

State context

Arizona recorded 281 pedalcyclist fatalities in FARS 2019–2024, ranking #5 among states by raw count. Raw counts favor large states: more residents, more riding, more exposure.

Adjusted for bike-commute share (a real but imperfect exposure proxy from ACS commute data — it misses recreational, delivery, and minors' riding), Arizona ranks #16 at 54.51 fatalities per 10,000 bike commuters (raw-count rank #4). The gap between the two ranks is itself informative.

What this data can and cannot say

  • Counts, not rates. No city-level ridership denominator exists; a higher count can simply mean more people ride.
  • Fatal motor-vehicle crashes only in the federal record — single-bike falls and non-fatal injuries (the majority of e-bike ED visits) are absent from FARS entirely.
  • E-bikes cannot be separated from conventional bicycles in federal crash data. Only CPSC's NEISS isolates them (2024 onward), as national emergency-department estimates that cannot be mapped to a city.
  • Only ~two-thirds of federal records are coded to a specific city — city counts are a floor, not a ceiling.
  • Crash-type codes describe what the report found, not fault.

Methodology & sources

Fatality data: NHTSA Fatality Analysis Reporting System (FARS), national files 2019–2024, pedalcyclist person types, age-banded (never exact ages). State exposure adjustment: ACS bike-commute share. Full methodology, source documentation, and all code are public: https://github.com/jamesad923-coder/ebike-crash-intel — every number in this report can be independently reproduced.
