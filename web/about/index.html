🚲 Crash Atlas

Our Story

Crash Atlas is an independent, open-data project that maps and explains U.S. bicycle and e-bike crash data — federal fatality records, national injury estimates, city open data, and news-detected incident reports — in one place, with every number traceable to its source.

It exists because of a gap: e-bike safety is being debated in city councils, school boards, and living rooms across the country, but the real data is scattered across half a dozen agencies, lags by years, and is easy to misread. Those debates deserve better than anecdotes — and better than overconfident dashboards that hide their own limitations.

The rules this project runs on

  • Every number is sourced. Each page names its dataset, links to it, and the full pipeline is open source — anything here can be independently reproduced.
  • Counts are not rates. Where no ridership denominator exists, we say so instead of implying risk comparisons the data can't support.
  • Limits are stated up front. Federal data can't separate e-bikes from bicycles; single-bike falls aren't in fatality data; news detection is automated and unverified. Every page says what its data cannot tell you, before it tells you anything.
  • No black boxes. Analyses here are interpretable and auditable — screening that supports engineering judgment, never a "prediction" that replaces it.
  • Privacy by design. Age bands, never exact ages; no names, ever — even when a news source prints them. The only measurement on this site is GoatCounter: anonymous, cookieless page-view counts — no personal data, no ads, no cross-site anything, and it's open source too.

Who runs this

Hi — I'm James Adigun. I build and maintain Crash Atlas independently: the data pipelines, the dashboard, the city pages, and the reports all come from the same open codebase. I'm a high school student based in Wall, New Jersey, and a fellow e-bike rider. I started Crash Atlas because my friends and I have had many injuries and near-death experiences on our bikes — we love our bikes, but we fear that any day we could lose one another to the things we love, just as so many others have. For that very reason, Crash Atlas has grown into a full-time effort to give cities, schools, families, and reporters the honest data layer this conversation has been missing — heightening awareness and, hopefully, helping create the infrastructure for a safer world for all riders.
